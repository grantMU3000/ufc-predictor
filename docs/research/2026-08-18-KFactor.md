## Key ideas
Source 1
- Elo converts the difference in two players' ratings into the expected outcome of a match
    - adjusts those player ratings based on how the match actually plays out relative to the expected outcome
- In Elo, based on the equation, every 400 points of advantage multiplies their odds of winning by 10
    - 2000-rated player has 100-to-1 odds of beating a 1200-rated player
- A small K is appropriate for established elite players, and a big K is good for newer/improving players
- K factor is adjusted dynamically within many systems
    - New players use K >= 40 so ratings converge to a reasonable estimate quickly
    - Established players typically have K as 10-20 to prevent single upsets from distorting a long track record
    - K can be decayed with games played, so long time players have stable ratings
- Upsets cost more than expected wins gain, which makes Elo self-correcting
- Sports use Elo with modifications 
    - Score of the match/margin of victory is a multiplier & bonus
- Online games use Glicko-2 & TrueSkill, which are good for multi-team games & introduce rating uncertainty
- Limitations:
    - No uncertainty estimate, which is a big deal since a new player with the same rating as an experienced player is less reliable for the new player
    - Ratings can inflate/deflate if new players consistently enter at fixed ratings & improve by beating established players. Elo would have to recalibrate
    - Elo assumes a one-dimensional skill hierarchy, so if player A beats B, B beats C, and C beats A, it struggles. This is common in fighting games
    - Margin of victory's ignored
- Glicko addresses uncertainty by tracking Rating Deviation alongside the rating
    - Players who haven't played recently have a high RD, and their ratings are updated more aggressively
    - It's essentially like a confidence interval
- Glicko-2 has a volatility parameter on top of rating deviation, which tracks how erratic a player's results have been
- Elo Ratings are zero-sum within a closed pool
    - Every gained point by one player is lost by another, so the average rating stays constant unless a player enters or leaves
- Elo isn't a measure of skill. It's a measure of recent performance against the rated pool
    - Two players in different countries who don't play in each other's pool aren't directly comparable through Elo
