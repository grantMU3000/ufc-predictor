## Key ideas
- Elo is now used for power rankings in soccer, esports matchmaking, and sports-betting models 
- A good rating system summarizes a team/player's skill with a single, interpretable number. It also updates as new games happen
- The gap between two team ratings maps to a win probability through a logistic curve
- After each game, the winner takes points from the loser (Bigger swing when it's an upset)
- K factor is a learning rate that controls how aggressively ratings chase the latest evidence
- Elo's formula can a home field bonus in its equation
- If a team wins when it's expected to, its rating barely changes. If they get an upset, the term is large & positive. The K factor scales how big these jumps are
- To build this, have all fighters a common rating (usually 1500), and then process all games sequentially
    - Tune K as you build the system to minimize predictive loss
- Cleanest way to treat Home field advantage is as a second tuning parameter alongside K
- Resist urge to set K very high for "fast reactions" because larke Ks chase noise
- Low K (8-16) leads to slow, stable ratings. Best for mature leagues with steady rosters, but it's slow to catch real improvement
- Medium K (20-32) is balanced in terms of responsiveness, and is best for most team sports/seasons
- High K (40+) causes fast, jumpy ratings. They're best for short tournaments, and are volatile. The problem is they overreact to single results.
- Assess the Log loss based off of the tuned K-factor (Wherever the log loss bottoms out at is the best K-factor)
- Compare estimated Elo vs True strength. If Elo follows the true strength diagonal, then you know your Elo system is valid
- Log loss rewards calibrated probabilities, not just correct calls
    - Lower scores are better. Log loss punishes confidently wrong models
- Cold start bias trap cause early-season ratings to be meaningless until each team plays a handfull of games (Since their ratings are all the same)
    - A fix is a higher K-rating for the first few games before tuning it downward. This lets ratings find their level quickly
- Including margin of victory adds important context to make ratings move more/less towards their true place
- Elo is a closed-pool method. You can't compare ratings across leagues unless these separate league teams play against each other
- K factor is a learning rate, so tune it by backtesting. Never by intuition
