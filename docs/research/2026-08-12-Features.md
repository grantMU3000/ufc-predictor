## Key ideas
Source 1
- Wants to have basic fighter statistics (e.g. avg strikes landed)
- Use lag functions to prevent data leakage (e.g. future fight results to predict current fight)
- Ensured fighter styles were different based on weight class
- Using group bys for feature engineering can make your scripts slower
- Considered amount of rounds as veteranship, and considers damage for impacting the future
- Using differentials can help with determining skill (e.g. how often are they hit relative to how much they're not hit)
- His model is filtered more towards experienced fighters since there's more data for them
- His model isn't the best for linear regression because of the amount of dimensions he uses
- Incorporates an elo model, and wants to outperform it. Otherwise, it's pointless
- Weight class may be added later within his features, but he thinks it will be redundant
- Uses elo to measure skill 
- Took submissions into account by combining amount of takedowns w/ amount of submissions to determine how good they are at subs
- Fighter rows will be duplicated to account for each fighter

Source 2
- A good vs great model comes down to feature engineering
- Making features that capture the contextual nature of sport performance is the biggest challenge
- Standard statistics provide the foundation, but they miss the most influential factors of performance
- Optimize for efficiency because these lookups could take a while to do for predictions
- Group similar fighters, and assess how fighter A performs against that group
- Situational context needs to be considered
- Use real-time APIs when necessary to calculate factors (e.g. Weather for baseball)
- Rolling averages treat all recent games equally, but using exponential decay highlights the importance of recent performances, and how specific skill assessments vary by type (14-day half-life vs 28-day)
- Need to be able to capture genuine patterns instead of random noise (especially for streaks/rythms)
- He attempted to capture how features interact multiplicatively rather than additively (Power x Environment) 
- Analysis time can take hours (He looked at 400,000+ batter-game combinations), so you need to find ways to optimize
- Situational context appeared to matter the most
- Feature engineering is highly iterative (Each category reveals new insights that influence subsequent categories)
